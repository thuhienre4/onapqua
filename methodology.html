REVIEW METHODOLOGY

How we research and score software

Last updated: November 4, 2026

Our reviews are research-based. We combine official product information with independent user and expert feedback, then summarize it in a clear verdict you can act on.

1. Product research

We read the product's documentation, feature pages, changelog and live pricing page, and note plan limits, refund policies and free versions or trials.

2. Verification

We cross-check the vendor's claims against independent sources: user reviews on Trustpilot, G2, Capterra and WordPress.org, and established tech publications. We look for patterns across many reviews rather than single opinions. Every review links to the sources used.

3. Evaluation criteria

  • Features: does it do the job well, and how complete is it?
  • Ease of use: setup, interface and learning curve.
  • Pricing & value: cost versus alternatives, renewals, refunds and free plans.
  • Support & reputation: support quality, update frequency and what customers report.

4. What our scores mean

  • 4.5–5.0 Outstanding: easy to recommend for most people in its category.
  • 4.0–4.4 Very good: a strong choice with minor trade-offs.
  • 3.5–3.9 Good: worth it for specific needs; read the cons.
  • Below 3.5 Mixed: notable issues reported; consider alternatives first.

Where a review shows a score breakdown, each criterion is scored on the same 5-point scale.

5. Rankings and comparisons

"Best software" lists and comparison tables are ordered by our review scores. Affiliate relationships do not change the order.

6. Keeping reviews current

We revisit reviews when vendors change pricing or features, and recheck coupon codes on the official sites. The "Updated" date shows the last change.
